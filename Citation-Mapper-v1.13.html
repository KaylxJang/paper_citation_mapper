What does similarity mean?

Similarity is based on shared bibliographic references, not the number of times two papers directly cite each other or how similar their abstracts sound.

Score = shared references / √(references in A × references in B). For example, two papers with 10 references each and 5 shared references have a score of 0.50.

A score of 0–1 describes reference overlap in the indexed data. Missing bibliographies can produce low or absent scores; no line does not prove papers are unrelated. Dashed edges are limited by the similarity threshold and graph sparsification.
